# 1. Purpose
Defines when and how supplier contracts are renewed, renegotiated, or allowed to lapse.

# 2. Renewal Notice Period
Procurement must notify a supplier of intent to renew, renegotiate, or terminate at least 90 days
before contract expiry. Contracts with annual spend above $250,000 require 120 days' notice and
Operations Director sign-off on the renewal decision, not just Procurement.

# 3. Renegotiation Triggers
A contract is flagged for early renegotiation (before the standard renewal window) if the
supplier's SLA breach rate exceeds 25% over two consecutive quarters, or if the supplier's Vendor
Risk Score falls below 40 (see Vendor Onboarding Procedure Section 3).

# 4. Price Change Approvals
Any renewal that increases unit price by more than 5% over the prior term is treated as a
Purchase Order Change under Procurement Policy Section 6 -- the original approval chain re-runs in
full, even though the contract itself is new. Price decreases and increases of 5% or less follow
the standard single-level approval.

# 5. Auto-Renewal Prohibition
No Northstar contract may contain an automatic renewal clause. A contract that reaches expiry
without an explicit renewal decision lapses, and any subsequent orders to that supplier require
either a new contract or a spot-purchase Purchase Requisition following standard Procurement Policy
approval.

# 6. Termination for Cause
A contract may be terminated immediately (without the Section 2 notice period) if the supplier
has 3 or more Critical defects in a rolling 12-month period (Quality Control Procedure Section 3)
or fails a trial-order-equivalent re-qualification following a Corrective Action Request
(Quality Control Procedure Section 6).
